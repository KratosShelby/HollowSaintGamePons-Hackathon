# Hollow Saint

**Theme:** Something's Off
**Protagonist:** Dutch Marlowe — a journeyman monster hunter from the American Midwest. Not famous, not special, just a guy who does this for a living.

## Intro (a few lines, no new systems)

Dutch is on a routine layover through Baku — a few hours between flights, nothing planned. The flight gets grounded (a mechanical fault, or a freak storm — mundane, not obviously supernatural) and the rebooking keeps slipping a day at a time. Stuck in a cheap hotel with nothing to do, he picks up the local paper out of boredom. One headline doesn't sit right with him. He's got The Book in his bag anyway. Might as well.

This single beat justifies:
- Why a stranded traveler has time to read newspapers and go investigate
- The newspaper-as-hub mechanic
- Re-reads differently once the twist lands: his flight "just happening" to keep getting delayed, in the city where the final case turns out to be about him.

## Location

**Hotel room, 414 (home base + hub):** where Dutch sleeps, reads the paper, and the mirror lives. Small, cramped, venetian-blind light stripes across the room (ref #22). A cafe/second-location split was considered and dropped — not worth the added scope this late; everything happens in this one room until the final case sends him right back into it.

## Core loop (per case)

1. **Newspaper (hub):** Each day opens on the newspaper in Dutch's room — a few flavor headlines (mundane, atmosphere only) plus one bolded story that's the real lead, with a time/location detail baked into the text (e.g. "reported near 2 AM, Harbor Street"). Clicking the real story sends Dutch to that scene.
2. **Investigate:** interact with clue objects in the scene (wounds, residue, disturbed items) → each logs a "symptom" to the case file.
3. **The Book:** open the bestiary, cross-reference collected symptoms against entries → identify the creature.
4. **Select method:** each entry has a specific (sometimes absurd) weakness tied to its lore — not a generic "hit it 3 times."
5. **Confront:** apply the correct tool/method → resolve (success/fail).

## Bestiary (3 cases + the twist)

### Case 1 — The Famished (zombie-type)
- **Location:** Harbor Street Diner, the kitchen
- **Symptoms:** cold rot smell, flickering electronics, claw marks on the inside of doors
- **Weakness:** a salt line — but specifically rock salt from the kitchen where it died, not any salt
- **Tone:** grounded, a little grim

### Case 2 — The Echo (spirit)
- **Location:** Lantern Lane, a third-floor flat
- **Symptoms:** repeated whispers, objects slightly moved from where they were left, cold spots near mirrors
- **Weakness:** bound to a forgotten possession — a silver spoon from its own set, found in the scene, not brought from outside
- **Tone:** melancholy, a little funny (the specificity of "has to be ITS spoon" plays as dry humor)

### Case 3 — The Lodger
- **Location:** Caravanserai Inn, Room 9
- **Symptoms:** clocks stopped at the same minute, cigarette burns with no smoker, a bed made every morning nobody's slept in
- **Weakness:** (left unresolved in-fiction — the signs start matching Dutch's own room before the fix is written down)
- **Tone:** the half-match to Dutch's own habits (his stuck watch, his full ashtray, his unslept bed) plants here, reads as coincidence on first playthrough

### Case 4 — The Mirror-Born (final case, the twist)
- **Location:** back in Room 414 — his own hotel room
Dutch opens The Book expecting a new entry — and it's already written. In his own handwriting. Describing him. No tool works because the "weakness" the Book lists for this one is just: *look in the mirror.* The deduction system the player has trusted for 3 cases turns out to have been building a case file on the player's character the whole time.

This directly satisfies the theme's own text: "a seemingly ordinary mechanic might be hiding something completely different."

## Scope discipline

- One scene per case, 2-3 clue objects each, one bestiary entry to match against at a time (not a full multi-entry lookup puzzle — keep matching binary/obvious once enough symptoms are collected, so it doesn't become a separate hard puzzle layer).
- One recurring NPC (optional, only if time allows after the core loop is solid) via the dialogue system.
- No day/night simulation — the newspaper's bolded headline is the only "time" mechanic, just text.
